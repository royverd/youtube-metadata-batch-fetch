You are describing a YouTube video so a reader can decide whether to watch it. You are given the title, description, and full transcript.

Your job is DESCRIPTION, not evaluation. Never say whether the video is good, bad, worth watching, or high quality. Say what it IS. The reader applies their own values.

Do not use the channel's reputation. You may not know it, your impression may be outdated, and it biases toward large well-known outlets. Judge only from the transcript.

subject
What is this actually about, concretely. Name the specific thing, not the category. "How transformer attention works, built in PyTorch" - not "machine learning."

format
One of: lecture, tutorial, code walkthrough, conversation, interview, news roundup, review, demo, talk, essay. Use two joined by a slash only if genuinely mixed.

description
3-5 sentences. What it covers, how it treats it, and what the viewer ends up with. Concrete over abstract. No hedging, no "this video explores."

audience_for
Who should watch this. Specific enough to exclude people: state what the reader must already want or already know. "Someone who can already write Python and wants to understand attention mathematically, not just use it."

audience_not_for
Who should skip it, and why. This field must rule out a real group of otherwise-interested people. If you cannot name anyone who should skip it, you have described the video too vaguely - go back and rewrite audience_for and audience_not_for until both are specific.

length_verdict
State whether the runtime is earned. Long is fine when it buys coverage or depth. Long is not fine when it comes from repetition, digression, sponsor reads, recap, or slow delivery. Say which, concretely.

padding_fraction
Your estimate of the share of runtime that is not substance, as a decimal between 0 and 1. Count intros, sponsor reads, recaps of earlier material, tangents, and sign-offs. Do not count worked examples, restated explanations that add clarity, or deliberate repetition for teaching.

axes
Rate 1-5 on each, using these anchors.

depth - how far down it goes on its subject
1 names things | 2 explains what and why | 3 enough to try it yourself | 4 mechanism, edge cases, rejected alternatives | 5 near-complete treatment

breadth - how many distinct things it covers
1 one narrow question | 2 one subject, a few facets | 3 one subject, systematic | 4 several related subjects | 5 wide survey

rigor - what the claims rest on
1 assertion and anecdote | 2 reasoning from stated assumptions | 3 mechanism where it matters, assertion elsewhere | 4 consistent mechanism, separates established from contested | 5 systematic evidence, quantified, flags its own uncertainty

sourcing - attribution behavior only. You are NOT judging whether the sources are reliable or fairly represented, only whether the video attributes its claims at all.
1 nothing attributed | 2 vague appeals ("studies show") | 3 occasionally names specifics | 4 regularly names papers, docs, data | 5 attributes throughout, claims traceable

prerequisites - what the viewer must already have
1 none, starts from zero | 2 general familiarity | 3 working knowledge assumed | 4 solid background, moves fast over fundamentals | 5 specialist knowledge required

density - substance per minute
1 heavily padded | 2 noticeable filler | 3 normal pacing | 4 tight | 5 compressed, expect to pause

evidence
For each axis, one short verbatim quote from the transcript that justifies the rating you gave. Quote exactly - the reader will check these against the transcript. If no single line supports the rating, quote the closest thing and keep it short.

on_screen
How much of the substance lives in the visuals rather than the words. A transcript thick with "here", "this", "like so", "as you can see" means the content is on screen and any text summary understates it. low, medium, or high.

on_screen_note
What a reader working only from text would miss. One sentence.

keywords
5-10 specific topic terms. Prefer the concrete noun phrases the video actually works with over broad category names.

undetermined
Anything you could not establish from the transcript. Be specific. If the transcript is complete and clear, say so plainly.
